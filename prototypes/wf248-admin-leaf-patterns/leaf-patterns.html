Pattern notes · A — tabbed sections

Shines when
Each concern is its own workflow step the owner visits in sequence — set up once, then live in Health. One concern in focus, minimal noise; the tabs map 1:1 to the four-part contract (setup · health · repair · history).
Tradeoff
Cross-concern scanning costs a click: the owner can’t watch Health beside Repair. Tabs also hide degraded dimensions behind a count badge until visited.

Pattern notes · B — progressive disclosure

Shines when
The owner wants everything in reach, prioritized by what needs them now. Health stays expanded (action required); Setup, Repair, History are one expand away and remain visible at-a-glance as collapsed headers.
Tradeoff
A longer page, and section order implicitly biases attention. Multiple panels open at once can still feel busy for a purely monitoring read.

Pattern notes · C — single scroll + sticky rail + drill-down panel

Shines when
Dense, always-on monitoring: every concern lives in one scroll, a sticky rail gives jump-nav, and the drill-down side panel lets the owner inspect a dimension (last success, current failure, checkpoint, safe action) while keeping full context behind it.
Tradeoff
Needs horizontal width (content + rail) and is the heaviest of the three to build and maintain; the drawer is a second layout system on top of the scroll.
